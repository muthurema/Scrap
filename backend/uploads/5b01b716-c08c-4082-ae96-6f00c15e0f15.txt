Coordinate Reference Systems in GIS

A geographic coordinate system (GCS) uses a three-dimensional spherical surface to define
locations on the Earth using latitude and longitude, referenced to a datum such as WGS84.
A projected coordinate system (PCS) is based on a GCS but projects the curved surface onto a
flat plane using a map projection, giving Cartesian X and Y coordinates measured in linear units
such as metres.

The Universal Transverse Mercator (UTM) system divides the Earth into 60 longitudinal zones,
each spanning 6 degrees of longitude. Zone numbers increase eastward starting at the 180-degree
meridian. Within each zone, the transverse Mercator projection minimises distortion. UTM coordinates
are expressed as an easting and a northing in metres.

A datum defines the size and shape of the Earth and the origin and orientation of the coordinate
system. A datum transformation converts coordinates between two different datums, whereas a
reprojection converts between two coordinate systems that share the same datum.
